As usual, these opinions are mine, and while I will use terms like "we", and "us", and I do know people who have similar views or experiences, I also know I am not speaking for all autistics, because we are all different. 

A while after I was diagnosed with autism, someone said to me that "You seem to have gotten worse since your diagnosis." I've also heard someone talk about someone they know who was diagnosed saying "They are worse now than before the diagnosis, they can't go out without a backpack of fidget toys."

I was initially confused when I heard this. I was learning more about myself, why I do things the way I do, and what being autistic actually means on a day-to-day basis. Part of this process is slowly understanding which behaviours I'd unknowingly adopted over the decades to try to appear less weird, and to try to fit in. All of this takes energy, so dismantling some of these masking behaviours, especially in safer spaces, seems like a good thing. As a result, post-diagnosis I would have shown more autistic traits.

The biggest problem I have as an autistic is that society is not built in a way to accommodate autistics. I spend lots of money each week on psychology, psychiatry, speech pathology, behavioural support, and occupational therapy. I need these supports to help me navigate a society that is counterintuitive. One thing that I refuse to use them for is to help me fit in better. I do not need to mask more, I need to mask less. 

I am autistic. The way my brain works is that if you describe something I can spot potential problems. My instinct when I come to ask something is to ask, directly, and without needing to add any smalltalk. When I communicate, the words I use, and only the words, convey the meaning of what I say. When I'm feeling annoyed, or frustrated, or angry, it will show in my tone of voice and body language, but it will only convey that I am feeling annoyed, not that I'm annoyed at the person I'm talking to.

Sometimes I'm just overwhelmed, and I need something to take the edge off; ear plugs, fidgets, noice cancelling headphones, etc. Of course, not all fidgets are the same, they feel different, they provide different movements and they sooth me differently. Yesterday, I was out with several different fidgets, but the one that I instinctively needed to reach for at that time wasn't with me. With the help of my supports, and my improved self-understanding, I now understand this, while non-autistics may just see any fidget as just another fidget.

None of these behaviours are a problem for me. They are a problem for others. So when I decide that I'm going to be more authentic, that I'm going to reduce my masking a little, others see me as getting worse, not better. People who who make the statements that I opened with expect that the aim of my support is to change my behaviours, to try to understand the way their minds work, something which is as alien to me as their understanding the way my mind works, and my behaviours. 

I have [recently](% link _posts/2026-03-23-what-is-energy-for-autistics.md %) spoken about my limited energy, and I want to stop wasting it on appearing less autistic, or burying my authentic self to make others more comfortable, rather than spending more. Especially when, in general, the expectation is that the onus is on me to do all the work, without the expectation that they will have to put in any effort in understanding and accomodating my authentic self. 

If your expectation of someone's autism diagnosis is that they can get supports to help them fit in, you have decided that it's more important for them to pretend to not be autistic, than giving them a safe environment that they can learn to drop their masks, the problem is not the autistic, it's you. 

This World Autism Awareness Day, don't just be aware that autism exists. Remember, our brains work differently to non-autistics, the way we link thoughts, concepts and understandings. Remember that it's not our fault, and that we are not broken, we are happily autistic. Be aware of how autistics are treated when they stop masking, and do your bit to make society easier to manage, without expecting us to just adapt and change.
